# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-10-17

NIST took no official action this period, so the process news again comes from the community. The emphasis has moved from new forgery attacks to implementation security. The headline result is the first power-analysis attack aimed at SQIsign's Round-3 signing code: it finds five leakage points, each of which gives polynomial-time key recovery. The multivariate cryptanalysis continues with a new extension of the Schur–Macaulay framework to UOV over any finite field, which lowers attack-complexity estimates for some Round-3 QR-UOV parameter sets, and with a paper that supplies rigorous footing for the regularity assumptions behind UOV cryptanalysis. The rest is mostly Round-3 performance work (MAYO on Apple M1, SQIsign 3.0 with AVX-512, MQOM vectorization, FAEST v3 on GPUs), plus an 11-bit key-recovery loss for the withdrawn HAWK and a warning that in-the-head signatures need their relation encodings checked separately.

### [9/10] Power Analysis on SQIsign via Ideal Reconstruction from Quaternion Leakages
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Authors:** Won Kim, Jaeho Jeon, JeongHwan Lee, Suseong Lee, Young-Sik Kim, Changmin Lee (Korea University; DGIST)
- **Summary:** NIST treats side-channel resistance as a key Round-3 evaluation factor, but earlier side-channel work on SQIsign targeted the Round-1 design, used toy parameters, or assumed leakage that was never shown to be extractable. This paper identifies five side-channel vulnerabilities in the Round-3 signing procedure. For each one it gives a polynomial-time algebraic algorithm that recovers the secret key from the leaked quaternion and ideal values, including from partial-bit leakage. The authors then run a real power-analysis attack on the SQIsign source code to show that the leakage can actually be extracted. This puts concrete evidence behind the "signing is not yet constant-time" caveat that has followed SQIsign into Round 3.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2508

### [8/10] Extending the Schur–Macaulay Framework for UOV Attacks via Truncated Polynomial Algebras
- **Date:** 2026-10-11
- **Authors:** Peigen Li, Siyong Tao (Beijing Institute of Mathematical Sciences and Applications; Tsinghua University)
- **Summary:** This is the third paper in a row from these authors building on the Ding–Li–Tao wedge framework and Merz–Ran's Schur–Macaulay matrices. It recasts the Schur–Macaulay approach in polynomial terms and works in p-truncated polynomial algebras, so the method applies to the UOV problem over arbitrary finite fields. Divided powers and transfer relations supply extra public equations, which give quotient-dimension criteria for recovering parts of the hidden oil-space ideal. Applied to the third-round QR-UOV parameter sets, it gives lower attack-complexity estimates for some of them. That keeps the pressure on the UOV family's current parameters, not just retired ones.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2444

### [7/10] Genericity in Multivariate Cryptography
- **Date:** 2026-10-12
- **Authors:** Simon Abelard, Pierre Pébereau (KU Leuven), Mohab Safey El Din (LIP6, Sorbonne Université)
- **Summary:** Proves that key algebraic properties of UOV public keys are generic, specifically that UOV varieties are equidimensional and public-key ideals are radical. These are regularity assumptions that much of the published UOV cryptanalysis relies on, so the result puts those attack estimates on a firmer basis. The authors also review several multivariate schemes for places where non-genericity led to breaks, and give a counterexample whose non-genericity does not seem easy to exploit. This gives a design-level checklist that applies to UOV, MAYO, QR-UOV and SNOVA as they are tweaked during Round 3.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2452

### [6/10] Optimized NEON Implementation of MAYO on Apple M1
- **Date:** 2026-10-05
- **Authors:** Minwoo Lee, Minjoo Sim, Siwoo Eum, Hwajeong Seo (Hansung University)
- **Summary:** A generator-produced ARM NEON assembly implementation of Round-3 MAYO whose output matches the upstream NEON intrinsics code byte for byte. On an M1 Pro it speeds up compact-key signing by 1.99–2.12× and verification by 2.11–2.62× over upstream on all four parameter sets. A transposed, constant-time echelon form uses 55–70% fewer cycles, and eight-way interleaved AES expands the public matrix at 0.18 cycles/byte. The authors' statistical timing tests also flagged a difference in the MAYO2 signing test, and a key-dependent timing effect on public data in verification that upstream code shares. They say their test cannot rule out a secret-dependent cause, which is a useful lead for Round-3 constant-time review.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2350

### [5/10] The Lattice Isomorphism Problem with Hints
- **Date:** 2026-10-06
- **Author:** Mélissa Rossi (CryptoExperts)
- **Summary:** Builds a framework for LIP when an attacker has partial information about the secret, from side channels or by design. It embeds LIP into an SVP instance on a different lattice where hints can be added, and it converts hints on linear combinations of secret coefficients into usable form. Applied to Hawk, it estimates a key-recovery loss of about 11 bits for Hawk-1024 and covers hints from signatures as well as side-channel and fault-injection attacks. HAWK has already been withdrawn from the process, so this is mainly relevant to Hawk-derived work such as threshold variants and to LIP-based design in general.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2381

### [5/10] AVX-512 Acceleration of SQIsign 3.0 Verification
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Authors:** Yunqi Wen, Xuzhe Liu, Zhi Hu (Central South University); Saiyu Wang, Chang-An Zhao (Sun Yat-sen University)
- **Summary:** A low-latency AVX-512 implementation of full SQIsign 3.0 verification at security levels I, III and V. It vectorizes the curve-side work, including the Superglue transition, and uses dynamic-programming strategies for 2^ℓ-isogeny and theta-model (2,2)-isogeny chains. On an Intel i7-11800H the verifiers are 2.14×, 2.08× and 2.34× faster than the Round-3 Broadwell reference, which is already optimized assembly. An appendix covers key generation and signing. Verification cost is SQIsign's main performance weakness against the other candidates, so this narrows the gap on server-class x86.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2505

### [5/10] Efficient Vectorized Implementation of MQOM
- **Date:** 2026-10-10
- **Authors:** David Santos, Julio López (University of Campinas); Francisco Rodríguez-Henríquez (Technology Innovation Institute)
- **Summary:** A vectorized implementation of MQOM v2.1 for Arm NEON, x86 AVX2 and AVX-512. It uses a transpose-enabled paired-matrix layout to cut wasted SIMD work and memory traffic. The headline result is up to a 39.43% reduction in peak dynamic memory. Extra experiments on the Round-3 MQOM v3 code test whether the layout optimization still helps. Memory footprint matters for MPC-in-the-Head schemes on constrained devices, which makes this a relevant data point for MQOM in Round 3.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2439

### [4/10] FAEST on GPUs: Addressing Field Multiplication as a Bottleneck
- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Author:** Minseop Choi (NORMA, Inc.)
- **Summary:** An NVIDIA GPU implementation of FAEST key generation and the QuickSilver OWFProve and OWFVerify steps, covering the standard and EM variants in both s and f modes. Once AES is hardware-accelerated, binary-field multiplication takes about 82% of FAEST-128f signing time. Using the native PTX `clmad` carry-less instruction plus Karatsuba, the field-multiplication kernel reaches 26.6× the throughput of the software build at 256 bits, and full OWFProve reaches 2.9× (standard) and 3.1× (EM). This identifies where FAEST's expensive signing actually spends its time.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2489

### [4/10] GPU-Assisted FAEST v3 Signing: Challenge Grinding and CPU–GPU Pipelining
- **Date:** 2026-10-05
- **Authors:** Ha-Gyeong Kim, Si-Woo Eum, Seung-Won Lee, Min-Ho Song, Hwa-Jeong Seo (Hansung University)
- **Summary:** Offloads FAEST v3's challenge-grinding counter search to a GPU, with the CPU doing final acceptance checks in counter order, and pipelines CPU and GPU for batches of independent signatures. The output is bit-identical to the reference. The GPU path cuts single-signature latency for 4 of the 6 non-EM Round-3 parameter sets, by up to 1.60× for FAEST-192s, and wins 9 of 18 batch configurations on throughput. The authors conclude that the benefit is parameter-dependent and not uniform, which bears on how much the grinding added in Round 3 costs in practice.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2355

### [4/10] Practical Null-Branch Witness Attacks on In-the-Head Signatures
- **Date:** 2026-09-27
- **Authors:** Guoxiao Liu, Zhuoqing Peng, Guowei Liu, Keting Jia, Xiaoyun Wang, Zongyue Wang, Junfeng Fan
- **Summary:** A public-key-only universal forgery against AIMer v2.0, the AIM2-based scheme selected in Korea's KpqC competition, which succeeds with probability one on every parameter set (median 11.6 ms for AIMer-256f) and needs no signing queries. The attack works because zero factors in the encoded relation leave intermediate values unconstrained, so a witness can satisfy the relation without being a one-way-function preimage. The same idea extends to vulnerable VOLE-in-the-Head Lynxer variants. Neither scheme is a NIST Round-3 candidate, but the conclusion that relation encoding is a separate security obligation, beyond one-way-function hardness and proof-system soundness, applies directly to the in-the-head candidates FAEST, MQOM and SDitH.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2235
