# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-11-22

A quiet fortnight for the on-ramp process: NIST made no new official announcement, and no new Round 3 official comments turned up on pqc-forum. The main event was the PQC 2026 conference in Rennes (Nov 18–19), which scheduled a status report on the additional-signatures initiative and talks on SQIsign's post-attack parameter update and on the cryptanalysis that led to HAWK's withdrawal. No abstracts or slides for those talks have been posted yet. On the research side, the one substantive candidate paper is an efficiency improvement for dimension-4 SQIsign at the Round 3 primes, which claims the smallest Level I key-plus-signature size of any surviving post-quantum signature. The rest is a side-channel attack on AIMer, a related MPC-in-the-Head scheme.

### [6/10] Improving the Efficiency of 4-Dimensional SQIsign
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Authors:** Dustin Ray (Anchorage Digital), Paulo L. Barreto (University of Washington Tacoma)
- **Summary:** The verifier's dimension-4 isogeny chain in SQIsignHD respects an order-4 automorphism. The authors use this to recover two of the four kernel points from the other two, which cuts verification cost by 37–40%. The paper also proves the soundness of the verifier's image check and shows that the verifier must validate the forger-controlled basis hints. Implemented at the Round 3 SQIsign primes adopted after Wesolowski's attack, Level I signatures are 139 bytes (vs. 200 in the Round 3 spec) and key plus signature is 223 bytes. Verification is still about 4.3× slower than dimension-2 SQIsign. This is relevant to whether a compact dimension-4 variant is a viable tweak for the candidate.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2906

### [3/10] A Single-Trace Side-Channel Attack on the KpqC Digital Signature Scheme AIMer v3
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Authors:** Jinnuo Li, Yiteng Sun, Chi Cheng, Weijia Wang, Hao Cheng
- **Summary:** This is the first side-channel attack on AIMer v3, an MPC-in-the-Head signature selected as a finalist in the Korean KpqC competition. A deep-learning single-trace power analysis of AIM3's input affine layer recovers the full secret key from one signature on a Cortex-M4 with 100% success. AIMer is not a NIST Round 3 candidate, but the result is a warning for implementations of the MPCitH/VOLEitH candidates (FAEST, MQOM, SDitH), whose signing similarly processes secret witness bits through public linear layers.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2859
