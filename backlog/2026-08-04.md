# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-08-04

HAWK's withdrawal dominates the period: Claude Mythos Preview found a
key-recovery attack on HAWK-256, the team confirmed it and pulled the scheme
from Round 3 on July 29, leaving eight candidates. An independent trace-zero
cryptanalysis of HAWK's key recovery appeared the following day. The remainder
is implementation work — FPGA accelerators for MQOM and QR-UOV — plus a
VOLE-in-the-Head derivative built on Chinese block ciphers, all landing ahead of
the August 14 tweaks deadline.

### [10/10] Claude AI Cracks HAWK Post-Quantum Signature; Authors Withdraw from NIST Process
- **Date:** 2026-07-28
- **Authors:** Swati Khandelwal (The Hacker News)
- **Summary:** Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview discovered a previously unknown symmetry (nontrivial automorphism) in HAWK-256's lattice structure, enabling a key-recovery attack that reduces the work factor from 2^64 to 2^38, achievable in ~3 hours 42 minutes on a 96-core server. The HAWK team confirmed the findings and withdrew the scheme from NIST's third-round evaluation on July 29, stating that "straightforward mitigations would make HAWK uncompetitive." NIST updated its candidate list accordingly, leaving eight schemes in Round 3. The attack does not affect other candidates or deployed post-quantum standards.
- **Link:** https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/claude-ai-just-cracked-post-quantum.html

### [7/10] Dimension Reduction for SVP in Hawk: A Trace-Zero Approach
- **Date:** 2026-07-30
- **Authors:** Guilhem Mureau, Alice Pellet-Mary (Univ Bordeaux, CNRS, Inria, IMB)
- **Summary:** Independent of the Claude Mythos attack, this paper presents a new cryptanalytic approach to HAWK by reducing module lattice problems to a rank-3 module-LIP instance over the maximal totally real subfield of a cyclotomic field. The result is an algorithm that breaks HAWK's key recovery using polynomially many exact-SVP calls in lattices of dimension at most 3m/8+1, improving over prior approaches. While HAWK has already been withdrawn, this analysis contributes to the broader understanding of lattice-based signature security relevant to future proposals.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1560

### [5/10] MQ on my Hardware: Performance Analysis of MQOM on FPGA
- **Date:** 2026-07-23
- **Authors:** Stelios Manasidis, Quinten Norga, Suparna Kundu, Ingrid Verbauwhede (COSIC, KU Leuven)
- **Summary:** First dedicated hardware accelerator for MQOM, an MPC-in-the-Head signature scheme in NIST's Round 3. The FPGA implementation achieves 0.46 ms signature generation and 0.38 ms verification with minimal memory, demonstrating competitive performance against lattice-based ML-DSA. This work strengthens MQOM's case for practical deployment by addressing hardware-efficiency concerns ahead of the August 14 tweaks deadline.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1483

### [5/10] A High-Speed Hardware Accelerator for QR-UOV Signature Scheme
- **Date:** 2026-07-21
- **Authors:** Renma Sugai, Hiroshi Amagasa, Rei Ueno, Naofumi Homma
- **Summary:** Proposes a systolic-array-based hardware accelerator for QR-UOV covering key generation, signing, and verification. FPGA results show 0.74 ms keygen, 0.28 ms signing, and 0.19 ms verification at NIST security level I, outperforming existing UOV hardware implementations. Contributes to the practical-readiness argument for QR-UOV as Round 3 teams prepare their updated submissions.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1458

### [4/10] SM4th and uBlockith: VOLE-based Post-Quantum Signature Schemes from Chinese Block Ciphers
- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Authors:** Weihan Li, Yuchen Wang, Zhelei Zhou, Cheng Hong, Tao Wei (Ant Group)
- **Summary:** Adapts the FAEST VOLE-in-the-Head framework to Chinese-designed block ciphers SM4 and uBlock, producing two new signature families targeting 128-bit and 256-bit security. SM4th achieves performance comparable to FAEST-128 on platforms with native SM4 acceleration. While not a direct NIST candidate, this demonstrates the portability of FAEST's design and its potential for regional cryptographic ecosystems.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1506
