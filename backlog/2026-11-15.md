# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-11-15

A quiet fortnight for the process: no NIST announcements and no new official comments, and the Round-3 field stays at eight candidates after HAWK's withdrawal. The most important item is "Cryptanalysis of MinRank 2.0", co-authored by NIST's Ray Perlner and Daniel Smith-Tone, which improves MinRank attacks. MinRank underlies the analysis of UOV, MAYO, QR-UOV and SNOVA, although the abstract gives no new security levels for those four schemes. The rest is mainly implementation work: AVX-512 theta-model isogenies that speed up SQIsign Round-3 verification by about 3×, and a GPU implementation of MAYO. There are also a few tangential theory papers on isogeny algorithms, Fiat–Shamir seed trees, and a cubic UOV variant.

### [7/10] Cryptanalysis of MinRank 2.0
- **Date:** 2026-11-03
- **Authors:** Nicolas Aragon, Maxime Bros, Ray Perlner, Daniel Smith-Tone, Javier Verbel
- **Summary:** Revisits attacks on the MinRank problem, which the authors call "ubiquitous" in the cryptanalysis of the Round-3 multivariate candidates UOV, MAYO, QR-UOV and SNOVA. A kernel-search improvement cuts 3–6 bits on Mirath parameters and the Courtois challenges. The authors also solve Courtois challenge D, open since 2001, in 11,434 CPU-days (about 2^61 bit operations), and correct Support-Minors and Minors estimates for q=2 by up to hundreds of bits. The abstract gives no new security levels for the four multivariate candidates. Two of the authors are on NIST's PQC team, so the corrected estimates are likely to inform how NIST assesses multivariate security margins in Round 3.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2733

### [5/10] Vectorized (2, 2)-Isogenies in the Theta Model Using AVX-512 and Applications to SQIsign
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Authors:** Marius A. Aardal, Diego F. Aranha, Patrick Longa, Giacomo Pope
- **Summary:** Presents an AVX-512 implementation of two-dimensional isogenies in the theta model, with a code generator for arithmetic over primes of the form c·2^e − 1. Round-3 SQIsign verification runs 3.1–3.4× faster, and Round-2 verification 2.5× faster at Level I and 3.2–3.3× at Levels III and V. Slow verification is a recurring objection to SQIsign, for example on pqc-forum in DNSSEC discussions, so these speedups bear directly on its Round-3 performance case.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2773

### [5/10] SpreadMAYO: Unlocking Greater Parallelism for Faster MAYO Signatures on GPUs
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Authors:** Zhuoyu Xie, Tian Zhou, Fangyu Zheng, Yijing Ning, Yi Bian, Wangchen Dai, Jiankuo Dong, Yuewu Wang, Jingqiang Lin, Jiwu Jing
- **Summary:** A GPU implementation of Round-3 MAYO that increases signing parallelism. On an RTX 4090 it beats an adapted Round-3 GPU baseline by 2.33–3.71× in key generation, 4.40–6.93× in signing and 3.42–5.13× in verification, and by up to 10.75× for high-volume server signing with a repeated key. This adds performance data for MAYO's Round-3 parameters, but says nothing about the scheme's security.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2705

### [4/10] An Improved Algorithm to Solve the Isogeny Problem in Dimension 2 with Precomputation
- **Date:** 2026-11-06
- **Authors:** Maria Corte-Real Santos (ENS de Lyon, CNRS)
- **Summary:** Gives an unconditional algorithm for the superspecial isogeny problem in dimension 2. After a one-time precomputation that depends only on p, each instance takes expected time and memory p^(3/4+o(1)), and p^(19/23+o(1)) including precomputation, versus p^(1+o(1)) for Costello–Smith. The abstract does not name SQIsign or any candidate. It is background cryptanalysis for higher-dimensional isogeny techniques, not a direct attack on SQIsign, whose security rests on the dimension-1 endomorphism-ring problem.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2808

### [4/10] Revisiting Fiat-Shamir NIZKPs and Signatures from Seed-Friendly Sigma Protocols
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Authors:** Andrea Flamini, Federico Pintore, Edoardo Signorini
- **Summary:** Argues that fixed-weight parallel repetition and seed-tree compression improve security as well as proof size for Fiat–Shamir signatures built from seed-friendly Sigma protocols. With a salted seed tree, the standard Fiat–Shamir transform is straight-line extractable in the classical random-oracle model, with no extra proof field or proof-of-work. The abstract does not name any candidate. Seed trees and fixed-weight challenges are building blocks of several MPC-in-the-Head-style signatures, so the work may matter for proof debates such as the SDitH/MQOM comment thread.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2858

### [3/10] An Improvement to the KLPT2 Algorithm
- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Authors:** Abel Laval (Université Libre de Bruxelles)
- **Summary:** A short note that reduces the output size of KLPT2, the dimension-2 analogue of the quaternion KLPT algorithm, from O(p^15.5) to Õ(p^14.5). It also shows that the recent Nakagawa–Yoshizumi–Onuki improvement to KLPT is compatible with the Hermitian module splitting approach, bringing the bound to Õ(p^14). This is algorithmic background for higher-dimensional isogeny constructions, of the kind that underpin SQIsign-style designs, rather than direct input to the NIST process.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2862

### [3/10] On the Security of the Cubic UOV Variant CML
- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Authors:** Manit Gupta
- **Summary:** Breaks CML, a cubic multivariate signature scheme over F_(2^8) in the UOV family. The cubic homogeneous parts of its public key span a space of dimension at most two, which the attack uses to recover an equivalent key. Key recovery took under 5 minutes for every tested parameter set, including the one targeting 256-bit security. CML is not a NIST candidate. The paper is a reminder that structured UOV variants keep falling to structural attacks, while plain UOV holds.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2872
