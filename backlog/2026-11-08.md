# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-11-08

This backfill picks up the primary sources behind the 2026 process milestones: NIST's 14 May pqc-forum announcement advancing nine candidates to Round 3, and the late-July pqc-forum thread where an improved key-recovery attack on HAWK led its team to withdraw. The main theme is cryptanalysis that cuts security margins without breaking schemes outright. Wesolowski's p^(1/3) supersingular-isogeny algorithm, now argued to hold unconditionally, created the parameter uncertainty SQIsign answered with larger Round-3 primes. On the multivariate side, a series of wedge-product, truncated-ring and pseudo-oil attacks lowered estimates for SNOVA, UOV, MAYO and QR-UOV. The rest covers official comments disputing the MPC-in-the-Head proofs for SDitH and MQOM, side-channel and fault attacks on SDitH, UOV and MAYO, and FAEST optimizations.

### [9/10] HAWK-n Key Recovery Reduces to SVP in Dimension n/2 + 1 (pqc-forum thread, incl. HAWK withdrawal)
- **Date:** 2026-07-28
- **Authors:** Steve Weis (first post); replies from Daniel Apon, Markku-Juhani O. Saarinen, Léo Ducas for the HAWK team, and others — NIST pqc-forum
- **Summary:** This is the primary forum thread announcing an AI-assisted (Claude) key-recovery attack that reduces HAWK-n to SVP in dimension n/2+1. In the gate-count model it lowers key-recovery cost for HAWK-512 from 2^150 to 2^108, and for HAWK-1024 from 2^288 to 2^182; a HAWK-256 key was recovered end to end. Later in the thread the HAWK team wrote that the obvious fixes (doubling parameters or moving to higher-rank modules) would make HAWK uncompetitive, and withdrew it. That removed the only lattice-based candidate from Round 3.
- **Link:** https://groups.google.com/a/list.nist.gov/g/pqc-forum/c/2r2u6SbHun4

### [9/10] Announcement of the 3rd Round Onramp Candidates (pqc-forum)
- **Date:** 2026-05-14
- **Authors:** NIST PQC team (Dustin Moody); replies from Thom Wiggers, John Mattsson, Daniel Apon, Bo Lin
- **Summary:** NIST's official forum announcement of the nine Round-3 candidates: FAEST, HAWK, MAYO, MQOM, QR-UOV, SDitH, SNOVA, SQIsign and UOV. It cites NIST IR 8610, sets a roughly two-year third phase that allows "tweaks", and plans the 7th PQC Standardization Conference for late spring or early summer 2027. In the discussion, Mattsson highlights IR 8610's line that NIST expects a longer timeline for any multivariate scheme and is unlikely to standardize one without a further round.
- **Link:** https://groups.google.com/a/list.nist.gov/g/pqc-forum/c/LXoTAe5AN78

### [8/10] The Supersingular Isogeny Problem in Time and Memory p^{1/3+o(1)}
- **Date:** 2026-07-20
- **Authors:** Benjamin Wesolowski (ENS de Lyon, CNRS)
- **Summary:** Under a smoothness heuristic, this paper solves the supersingular isogeny problem in time and memory p^{1/3+o(1)}, improving on the long-standing p^{1/2} bound. The abstract says the effect on concrete parameters "remains to be clarified" because of superpolynomial overhead and high memory use. This is the attack that pushed SQIsign onto larger primes in its Round-3 specification.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1486

### [8/10] Round 3 (Additional Signatures) OFFICIAL COMMENT: SDitH, MQOM
- **Date:** 2026-10-04
- **Authors:** Demi Marie Obenour; replies from John Mattsson, Matthieu Rivain (for the SDitH and MQOM teams), Sydney Antonov, Uri Blumenthal — NIST pqc-forum
- **Summary:** An official Round-3 comment arguing that the security proofs of SDitH and MQOM rest on the ideal cipher model, which AES-192/256 do not satisfy because of related-key and known-key distinguishers. Rivain replies that the assumption can be relaxed to concrete assumptions on AES-128 and Rijndael-256-256, and cites a correlated-tree analysis in ePrint 2026/615 (ASIACRYPT 2026). The thread is the main open proof-model debate for the two MPC-in-the-Head candidates.
- **Link:** https://groups.google.com/a/list.nist.gov/g/pqc-forum/c/gWeL5Oiim-s

### [8/10] Round 2 (Additional Signatures) OFFICIAL COMMENT: SNOVA
- **Date:** 2026-02-24
- **Authors:** Hung Le, Bros, Lichtinger, Minaud, Perlner, Smith-Tone, Valenzuela; reply from Po-En Tseng for the SNOVA team — NIST pqc-forum
- **Summary:** The authors extend their wedge-product attack to lower the security of 8 of SNOVA's 11 parameter sets. SNOVA-V (29, 6, 16, 5) falls from about 310 to about 181 bits. The SNOVA team accepts the result and commits to raising the number of vinegar variables. It also notes that Furue and Ikematsu's new attack affects UOV, which framed the multivariate parameter changes going into Round 3.
- **Link:** https://groups.google.com/a/list.nist.gov/g/pqc-forum/c/z3v13roERpU

### [7/10] Pseudo-Oil Subspaces and the Geometry of Underdetermined MQ Problems
- **Date:** 2026-06-01
- **Authors:** Massimo Ostuzzi (Ruhr University Bochum)
- **Summary:** The paper reinterprets Hashimoto's algorithm as computing a "pseudo-oil subspace". It then combines extra Gröbner-basis steps, linearization and combinatorial search to cut the cost of the direct attack on Security Level I parameters by 7 bits for MAYO and 8 bits for QR-UOV. It is accepted to ASIACRYPT 2026, and its techniques underpin the later geometric forgery attack on MAYO.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1122

### [7/10] Key Recovery Attacks on UOV Using p^ℓ-truncated Polynomial Rings
- **Date:** 2026-02-18
- **Authors:** Hiroki Furue (NTT), Yasuhiko Ikematsu (Kyushu University)
- **Summary:** This paper gives a framework for recovering UOV's secret oil subspace over F_{p^e} by running XL in p^ℓ-truncated polynomial rings, and adds new reconciliation and intersection attacks. The intersection attack reduces several UOV parameter sets below their claimed security levels. A SNOVA analysis in an earlier version was withdrawn. It appears at CRYPTO 2026 and started the truncated-ring line of attacks on odd-characteristic UOV variants.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/298

### [7/10] Syndrome Decoding out of the Head: Structure-Aided Side-Channel Witness Recovery on SDitH
- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Authors:** Jaeyeon Lee, Jaeho Jeon, Young-Sik Kim (DGIST)
- **Summary:** A profiled power-analysis attack on the SDitH Round-3 reference implementation on ARM Cortex-M4. A CNN classifies the mux digits, and a syndrome-based decoder exploiting regular-syndrome-decoding structure corrects the errors. It recovers all 100 test witnesses under -Os with 4,000 profiling traces, and 91 of 100 under -O3, which puts side-channel hardening on the Round-3 agenda for SDitH.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2760

### [7/10] Smoothness of Shortest Isogenies to Frobenius Conjugates
- **Date:** 2026-10-29
- **Authors:** Yi Ouyang, Wei Wei (USTC), Zheng Xu (Hefei National Laboratory)
- **Summary:** This paper bounds the smoothness probability of the shortest isogeny from a random supersingular curve to its Frobenius conjugate. The authors say this makes Wesolowski's p^{1/3+o(1)} attack unconditional. It removes the heuristic caveat from the result behind SQIsign's Round-3 reparameterization.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2677

### [6/10] Just Guess: Improved (Quantum) Algorithm for the Underdetermined MQ Problem
- **Date:** 2025-09-30
- **Authors:** Alexander May, Massimo Ostuzzi, Henrik Ressler (Ruhr University Bochum)
- **Summary:** This paper reduces underdetermined MQ to a sequence of univariate quadratics plus exhaustive guessing, which Grover's algorithm then speeds up quadratically. The abstract says this lowers the quantum security of every security level of MAYO, QR-UOV and SNOVA. It appears at EUROCRYPT 2026 and is part of why NIST signalled a longer timeline for the multivariate candidates.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1788

### [6/10] Weak Keys Break the BUFF Security of HAWK
- **Date:** 2026-06-21
- **Authors:** Quang Dao (Carnegie Mellon University; LayerZero Labs)
- **Summary:** HAWK's reference verifier accepts degenerate public keys under which the all-zero signature verifies. That breaks the message-bound signature, malicious strong universal exclusive ownership and weak non-resignability properties that HAWK claimed "as is". The paper proposes a norm-floor check as a partial fix. It was posted a month before the key-recovery attack that ended HAWK's candidacy.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1298

### [6/10] Improved Attacks for SNOVA by Exploiting Stability under a Group Action
- **Date:** 2024-10-30
- **Authors:** Daniel Cabarcas, Peigen Li, Javier Verbel, Ricardo Villanueva-Polanco
- **Summary:** This paper exploits stability of SNOVA systems under a commutative matrix group to speed up the reconciliation attack on all parameter sets by factors of 2 to 2^20. Its forgery attack takes some SNOVA parameter sets below NIST's threshold. It was presented at CRYPTO 2025 and was a key reason SNOVA revised its parameters during Round 2.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1770

### [6/10] Solving the Supersingular Isogeny Problem in Time p^{2/5+o(1)} Using Bivariate Multipoint Evaluation
- **Date:** 2026-07-31
- **Authors:** Aleksei Udovenko (University of Luxembourg)
- **Summary:** This follow-up to Wesolowski gives an unconditional p^{2/5+o(1)} algorithm for the supersingular isogeny problem. The author says it is of theoretical interest and does not threaten isogeny cryptosystems in practice. It is useful context for judging how much margin SQIsign's Round-3 primes need.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1575

### [5/10] NIST Advances Nine Post-Quantum Signature Algorithms to Third Round
- **Date:** 2026-05-21
- **Authors:** Matt Swayne — The Quantum Insider
- **Summary:** Trade-press coverage of NIST's Round-3 selection, listing the nine candidates after about 18 months of Round-2 evaluation. It notes the expected two-year third phase and the chance for teams to submit tweaked specifications and implementations. It is a readable summary for people outside the cryptography community.
- **Link:** https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/05/21/nist-advances-nine-post-quantum-signature-algorithms-to-third-round/

### [5/10] Shorter, Tighter, FAESTer: Optimizations and Improved (QROM) Analysis for VOLE-in-the-Head Signatures
- **Date:** 2026-01-31
- **Authors:** Carsten Baum, Ward Beullens, Lennart Braun, Cyprien Delpech de Saint Guilhem, Michael Klooß, Christian Majenz, Shibam Mukherjee, Emmanuela Orsini, Sebastian Ramacher, Christian Rechberger, Lawrence Roy, Peter Scholl
- **Summary:** The FAEST team replaces hash-based vector commitments with an AES-based PRG and compresses the AES witness using Galois theory. Signing and verification get up to 50% faster, signatures get smaller, and security stays the same. The paper also gives a tighter classical proof and a QROM proof with a new Fiat–Shamir transform. These optimizations feed into FAEST's later specification versions.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/164

### [5/10] Revisiting the Wedge Attack on UOV Problem
- **Date:** 2026-07-29
- **Authors:** Jintai Ding, Peigen Li, Siyong Tao
- **Summary:** This paper recasts the wedge attack in an algebraic-geometric framework and extends it to multi-homogeneous systems over fields of any characteristic. It then applies the result to SNOVA. It continues the wedge-product line that has driven parameter changes across the UOV family.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1548

### [5/10] Oil, Vinegar, and Sparks: Key Recovery from UOV via Single Electromagnetic Fault Injection
- **Date:** 2026-01-30
- **Authors:** Fabio Campos, Daniel Hahn, Daniel Könnecke, Marc Stöttinger
- **Summary:** An electromagnetic fault-injection attack on the randomized UOV implementation. With one fault-free signature and one faulty signature, it recovers the full secret key, succeeding more than 90% of the time on a real device. The authors discuss countermeasures, which bear on NIST's implementation-security evaluation of UOV.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/154

### [5/10] Secret-Subspace Recovery in MAYO via Linearization of Errors from a Single Fault
- **Date:** 2026-01-21
- **Authors:** Alberto Marcos (Arquimea Research Center)
- **Summary:** This paper shows that one injected fault during MAYO signing reveals a nonzero vector of the secret oil subspace. Recovery reduces to a linear system or a small quadratic system over the base field, and the attack also applies to randomized MAYO. It adds to a growing body of fault-attack work that MAYO implementations will need to defend against.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/097

### [4/10] QR-UOV without Rejection Sampling: Security Analysis and High-Speed Implementation
- **Date:** 2026-03-16
- **Authors:** Hiroshi Amagasa, Hiroki Furue, Rei Ueno, Naofumi Homma
- **Summary:** This paper proposes "NoRS QR-UOV", which maps rejected values to 0 during public-key expansion. The authors argue that the small coefficient bias this introduces leaves the claimed security levels intact, and verification at level I gets about 1.4× faster. Co-authored by a QR-UOV submitter, it is a candidate tweak for the scheme's Round-3 performance.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/527
