# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-08-16

The August 14 deadline for Round 3 updated submission packages has now passed, marking the start of the two-year third evaluation phase for the nine on-ramp signature candidates. The period's dominant theme is continued cryptanalytic pressure: a full-key-recovery attack on MQOM v2 was published days before the deadline, while a further reduction in HAWK key-recovery cost (from Anthropic researchers) adds to the body of work that led to HAWK's formal withdrawal from the process. On the constructive side, two independent teams delivered substantial performance improvements to SQIsign's core Qlapoti subroutine, and Siemens demonstrated a 97–99 % RAM reduction for MAYO verification on embedded devices.

### [9/10] Passive Full-Key Recovery for the MQOM v2 Lineage from Saltless Root Expansion
- **Date:** 2026-08-03
- **Authors:** José Luis Delgado (Universitat Oberta de Catalunya)
- **Summary:** Demonstrates that MQOM v2's use of a fixed PRG call with zero salt to derive roots from a master seed creates an exploitable vulnerability. When multiple signatures share these root functions, repeated master seeds generate linear equations that expose witness material, enabling full signing-key recovery and signature forgery. Published just days before the Round 3 updated-submission deadline, this puts pressure on the MQOM team to patch the flaw in their v2.1 update.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1542

### [8/10] HAWK-n Key Recovery Reduces to SVP in Dimension n/2 + 1
- **Date:** 2026-08-06
- **Authors:** Zygimantas Straznickas, Stephen A. Weis (Anthropic)
- **Summary:** Presents an unconditional, deterministic polynomial-time reduction from HAWK-n key recovery to the Shortest Vector Problem in dimension n/2 + 1. This lowers practical key-recovery costs for HAWK-512 from roughly 2^150 to 2^108 and includes a working implementation that recovers a HAWK-256 key in hours on standard hardware. The attack does not apply to Falcon or cyclotomic fields with certain conductor properties. HAWK has since been formally marked as withdrawn on NIST's Round 3 page.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1593

### [7/10] Qlapoty: Improved Analysis and Efficiency for Quaternionic Ideal to Isogeny Transformation
- **Date:** 2026-08-16
- **Authors:** Max Duparc (EPFL), Antonin Leroux (DGA-MI / IRMAR), Sina Schaeffler (ETH Zurich / IBM Research)
- **Summary:** Refines the Qlapoti algorithm used in SQIsign's signing pipeline, correcting analytical gaps in the original failure-probability analysis and introducing an improved norm-equation solver that achieves 6–9× speedups over Qlapoti's solver. End-to-end SQIsign NIST-level-2 signing sees 1.3–2.1× improvement. These gains are particularly important as SQIsign's relatively slow signing speed has been a recurring concern in the standardization discussion.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1700

### [6/10] Qlapoti+ and More: Optimizing Isogeny-based Signatures
- **Date:** 2026-08-12
- **Authors:** Yi-Fu Lai (Shanghai Jiao Tong University)
- **Summary:** Presents further enhancements to the Qlapoti procedure, achieving 1.5–4.5× speedups for the subroutine itself. Applied to SQIsign NIST variants and PRISM, the optimizations yield 1.17–2.05× key-generation speedups and 1.19–1.82× signing speedups across NIST security levels 1, 3, and 5. Orthogonal to the Qlapoty improvements, the two results can be composed for additional gains.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1640

### [6/10] MAYO Lite: a Low-RAM Implementation of the MAYO Signature Scheme
- **Date:** 2026-08-12
- **Authors:** Sven Bauer, Fabrizio De Santis, Florian Wilde (Siemens AG)
- **Summary:** Presents a memory-optimized MAYO implementation that reduces RAM consumption by 97–99 % compared to the reference implementation at the cost of 50–200 % longer runtimes. The approach incrementally expands the public key, reorders matrix calculations, and accumulates precomputed values directly. Tested within the PQM4 framework across MAYO1–5 parameter sets, the work demonstrates MAYO's viability on smart cards and other resource-constrained devices — an important consideration for Round 3 evaluation.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1634
