# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-09-07

A quiet period following the August 14 deadline for Round 3 tweak submissions, with research activity focused on tightening security estimates rather than unveiling dramatic new attacks. The main threads are continued cryptanalysis of the multivariate and isogeny families: a new unified framework for wedge attacks on UOV, a gap identified in SQIsign's formal security proof, and a modest improvement in solving Boolean MQ that nibbles at MQOM's security margins. With HAWK officially withdrawn and tweaked specifications now submitted, the field appears to be settling into the longer evaluation cadence NIST has outlined through 2027.

### [7/10] The Extended Wedge Attack
- **Date:** 2026-08-30
- **Authors:** John Baena, Javier Verbel, Luis Villota
- **Summary:** Introduces a dual decomposition of oil-and-vinegar polynomials that unifies previous wedge attack variants against UOV into a single algebraic-geometric framework, and proposes a generalized "extended wedge attack" with explicit conditions for recovering the secret oil space. The framework could sharpen security estimates for UOV and its relatives (QR-UOV, MAYO, SNOVA) as Round 3 evaluation proceeds.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1830

### [7/10] A Note on the Security Proof of SQIsign
- **Date:** 2026-08-24
- **Authors:** Maher Mamah, David Jao
- **Summary:** Identifies a square-root loss in the recent security reduction for SQIsign by Aardal et al., stemming from an imprecise min-entropy bound on the commitment. The authors show the min-entropy is O(1/p) — optimal but still implying an information-theoretic loss in the zero-knowledge simulation — and conclude that roughly two-thirds of the expected bit-security is preserved, suggesting different proof techniques may be needed to close the gap at current parameters.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1775

### [5/10] A Decrementally-Improved Algorithm for Boolean MQ
- **Date:** 2026-08-17
- **Authors:** Charles Bouillaguet, Julia Sauvage
- **Summary:** Combines the polynomial-method approach with Dinur's recent techniques to produce a new algorithm for solving Boolean MQ instances, achieving a modest 1–4 bit improvement over prior art for MQOM-relevant parameters. The paper shows that when MQOM uses certain optional speedup techniques, the effective security drops 3–4 bits below claimed levels — a small but non-trivial erosion for a Round 3 candidate.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1704
