# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-10-04

The dominant story of this fortnight is that cryptanalysis of the multivariate branch has stopped merely bruising retired Round-2 parameters and has begun landing on Round-3 ones: a new structural forgery attack on MAYO puts the MAYO2 Round-3 parameter set roughly 3 bits below its required security level, and the Merz–Ran divided-powers technique has been generalised from characteristic two to arbitrary characteristic and pointed at QR-UOV. No official NIST action was published in the period, so the process news is entirely community-driven. The remainder of the digest is isogeny-side work — a strongly-unforgeable PRISM variant built on SQIsign's Round-3 parameters and an existence analysis keyed to SQIsign v3.0 — plus the first sign of HAWK's afterlife as a threshold-signature base following its withdrawal, and incremental cryptanalysis and primitive design around the UOV family and VOLE-in-the-Head.

### [9/10] Geometric Forgeries: Structural Cryptanalysis of MAYO
- **Date:** 2026-09-28
- **Authors:** Massimo Ostuzzi
- **Summary:** The first structural forgery attack against MAYO, exploiting the geometry of the multivariate quadratic maps that MAYO's "whipping" transformation produces. The author observes that whipping amplifies polar cancellations, yielding subspaces with low-dimensional quadratic and polar images, and combines this with the Pseudo-Oil Attack and a descent to the binary field; a multi-target reduction for homogeneous MQ maps supplies the rest. Concretely it cuts roughly 18 and 16 bits from MAYO Round-2 Levels I and III, and — the part that matters for standardization — roughly 6, 4 and 2 bits from the Round-3 MAYO1, MAYO2 and MAYO3 sets, leaving MAYO2 about 3 bits short of its target. This is the most consequential result of the period: it is an attack on current, not superseded, parameters.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2247

### [8/10] Attacking UOV-based Signatures over divided power algebras
- **Date:** 2026-09-30
- **Authors:** Peigen Li, Siyong Tao (Beijing Institute of Mathematical Sciences and Applications; Tsinghua University)
- **Summary:** A direct follow-on to Merz and Ran's Schur–Macaulay work, which derived extra equations from divided powers of public polar forms in characteristic two. This paper extends that construction to finite fields of arbitrary characteristic and applies it to the security analysis of QR-UOV. The significance is less any single complexity figure than the generalization: it removes the characteristic-2 restriction that had limited the technique's reach across the UOV family, and brings QR-UOV explicitly into scope.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2272

### [7/10] ECLIPSE: Strongly Unforgeable Isogeny Signatures from the Prime-Degree Variant of PRISM
- **Date:** 2026-10-02
- **Authors:** Dustin Ray (Anchorage Digital)
- **Summary:** Implements the prime-degree signature construction sketched by PRISM's authors, targeting a known structural limitation of SQIsign: it cannot achieve strong unforgeability, because an auxiliary isogeny is not bound by the hash function. ECLIPSE removes that auxiliary isogeny and supplies the first complete implementation — verifier, wire format, and two independent code bases — reporting 206-byte signatures at NIST Level I, signing about 2.3x faster than the SQIsign reference and verification roughly 2x more costly (1.7x with pre-prepared keys). It builds on SQIsign's Round-3 parameters, though formal strong-unforgeability proofs are deferred to future work.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2312

### [6/10] Kettle: Short Post-Quantum Threshold Signatures from the HAWK Signature Scheme
- **Date:** 2026-10-04
- **Authors:** Calvin Abou Haidar, Daniel Escudero, Thomas Espitau, Clément Hoffmann, Kaoru Takemure, Mehdi Tibouchi, Hernán Darío Vanegas Madrigal
- **Summary:** A UC-secure threshold protocol producing specification-compliant HAWK signatures for any number of parties, with as few as 2 online rounds (about a dozen total) and 2 kB of online communication per party, plus an MP-SPDZ proof of concept. Its process relevance is as an epilogue to HAWK's withdrawal from Round 3 after analysis forced up its required dimensions: rather than attempt a rescue, the authors argue HAWK retains an overlooked advantage in MPC-friendliness, yielding threshold signatures more compact than ML-DSA- or Falcon-based alternatives. It is a claim about salvage value, not about HAWK's standing as a candidate.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2288

### [6/10] Filtered Supersingular Isogeny Counts and Exact-Coset Response Existence
- **Date:** 2026-09-20
- **Authors:** Ti-Hong Qin, Hong-Yu Tang, Zong-Bin Wang, Wen-Lun Pan
- **Summary:** Studies when bounded-degree supersingular isogenies exist under prescribed degree filters and exact torsion constraints, using the Brandt trace formula and Hecke estimates to establish regimes where certain isogeny paths exist with high probability and others provably do not. The authors separate two algebraic subgroups that are structurally identical but have differing response probabilities in specific parameter regimes. SQIsign v3.0 parameters are the stated application, making this parameter-selection-relevant groundwork for the Round-3 isogeny candidate rather than an attack.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1974

### [5/10] Lynx: Symmetric Primitive for Shorter and Faster VOLE-in-the-Head Signatures
- **Date:** 2026-09-28
- **Authors:** Lin Jiao, Hongsen Yang, Hongrui Cui, Yituo He, Yonglin Hao, Xiaojie Guo, Qunxiong Zheng, Jiang Zhang, Yu Yu, Kang Yang
- **Summary:** Proposes a symmetric primitive designed specifically to shorten and speed up VOLE-in-the-Head signatures, the framework underlying Round-3 candidate FAEST. Work of this kind matters to the process indirectly: FAEST's competitiveness against the multivariate and isogeny candidates rests largely on the cost of its one-way function, so primitive-level gains translate into candidate-level ones. Listed under secret-key cryptography rather than cryptanalysis — this is construction work, not a security result.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1099

### [4/10] Breaking the Spell: Cryptanalysis of VDOO
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **Authors:** Max Cartor, Ryann Cartor, Ansta Rakotondrafara
- **Summary:** Cryptanalysis of VDOO, an oil-and-vinegar-family multivariate signature scheme outside the Round-3 set, demonstrating parameter failures and introducing a hybrid variant of the MinRank attack. The relevance to the on-ramp is methodological rather than direct: MinRank-style attacks are the shared toolkit against UOV, MAYO, QR-UOV and SNOVA, so a new hybrid formulation is worth tracking even when its immediate target is not a candidate. Included at low weight accordingly.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2178
