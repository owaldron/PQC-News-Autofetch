# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-08-09

Quiet period as teams prepare updated Round 3 submissions (deadline: August 14,
2026). HAWK remains withdrawn; 8 candidates continue. Two new ePrint papers
target Round 3 schemes and one industry article analyzes HAWK-withdrawal
migration impact.

### [8/10] Extending the Applicability of Algebraic Key Recovery Attacks on the UOV Signature Scheme
- **Date:** 2026-08-05
- **Authors:** Yasuhiko Ikematsu (Kyushu University), Hiroki Furue (NTT Social Informatics Laboratories)
- **Summary:** Presents a method to overcome limitations in existing algebraic key recovery attacks on UOV when additional kernel elements appear in the ideal generated by public polynomials. The authors show that their extended attack reduces the claimed security of certain SNOVA parameter sets — notably achieving an estimated security of only 2^103 gate operations for one configuration — directly relevant as teams finalize Round 3 specifications before the August 14 deadline.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1620

### [7/10] AES-Based Grinding for MPC-in-the-Head Signatures
- **Date:** 2026-08-06
- **Authors:** Matthieu Rivain (CryptoExperts)
- **Summary:** Proposes replacing Keccak with AES for the proof-of-work grinding step in the Fiat-Shamir transform used by FAEST, MQOM, and SDitH — the three MPC-in-the-Head schemes in NIST's Round 3. Since AES is faster than Keccak on modern CPUs and is already integral to these schemes' designs, the technique could meaningfully reduce signature generation time. Timely as all three teams prepare updated Round 3 submissions.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1625

### [5/10] AI Just Broke a Post-Quantum Signature Scheme Candidate — Here Is What That Means for Your Migration
- **Date:** 2026-07-30
- **Authors:** Samuel Tseitkin (Exequantum)
- **Summary:** Industry analysis of HAWK's withdrawal after Claude Mythos Preview found a structural weakness in ~60 hours of semi-autonomous work. Argues that the event validates the NIST process but underscores that "algorithm agility is no longer optional" — organizations should prioritize cryptographic asset discovery and build control-plane architectures that allow rapid algorithm rotation rather than relying on static algorithm selection. Notes that finalized NIST standards (ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA) remain unaffected.
- **Link:** https://www.exequantum.com/insights/ai-just-broke-a-post-quantum-signature-scheme-candidate.-here-is-what-that-means-for-your-migration
