# NIST PQC Signatures — 2026-09-27

The Round-3 "tweak" wave finally became visible this period: updated specifications and reference implementations for SQIsign, SDitH, UOV, SNOVA, FAEST, MQOM and MAYO are now public and benchmarked side by side, and almost every scheme paid for its round-2 cryptanalysis in bytes or in parameters. The dominant theme is that the tweaks did not buy quiet — Merz and Ran's Schur–Macaulay attack defeats all but one round-2 parameter set across the entire UOV family and pushed several alternate MAYO sets below their claimed security level, while Beullens and Hess recovered SNOVA secret keys from a biased vinegar sampler present in the Round-3 submission itself. SQIsign is the period's busiest scheme in the other direction: its v3.0 spec adopts new, larger primes in response to Wesolowski's p^(1/3) isogeny-path algorithm, and three separate papers work on whether that attack really bites and on clawing back the lost performance. The remainder is implementation and efficiency work — a compact dimension-4 SQIsign format, faster quaternion-ideal algorithms, and area-efficient SDitH-v2 hardware.

### [10/10] Attacking UOV-based Signatures with Schur–Macaulay Matrices
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **Authors:** Simon-Philipp Merz (ETH Zurich), Lars Ran (Radboud University Nijmegen)
- **Summary:** Introduces "divided powers" to generate extra equations beyond the original system and generalizes Macaulay matrices to arbitrary algebraic shapes, yielding attacks that break every round-2 parameter set of UOV, MAYO, SNOVA and QR-UOV except one. Four alternate MAYO parameter sets fall below their claimed security level while remaining out of reach of all previous attacks, and the technique extends to odd characteristic, removing the spurious kernel elements that had limited earlier SNOVA attacks. The results were disclosed to the MAYO team in advance and caused a Round-3 parameter set to be discarded and others raised — this is the single most consequential cryptanalytic result of the period for the multivariate candidates.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2181

### [9/10] Recovering SNOVA Secret Keys from Biased Vinegar Sampling
- **Date:** 2026-09-22
- **Authors:** Ward Beullens, Basil Hess (IBM Research – Zurich)
- **Summary:** The Round-3 SNOVA signer samples vinegar variables in F_q by reducing uniform byte strings modulo a power of q, and the resulting bias leaks secret-key material for the six odd-characteristic alternative parameter sets. Even at a maximum of 0.086 bits per variable, the leakage reduces to a q-ary LPN problem solvable with BKW plus Fourier hypothesis testing; the authors recover keys for four parameter sets from 9–180 million signatures in under 16 minutes. The recommended q=16 sets are unaffected, and the flaw is fixable by sampling vinegar variables uniformly — but it is a defect in the Round-3 submission, not a legacy issue.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2154

### [9/10] Round-3 spec and implementation round-up (nist-sigs-zoo PR #56)
- **Date:** 2026-09-18
- **Authors:** Thom Wiggers / PQShield
- **Summary:** Consolidates the Round-3 specifications, reference implementations and fresh AWS benchmarks for seven candidates, giving the first apples-to-apples view of what the tweak deadline actually changed. SQIsign 3.0 signatures grew (level I: 148→200 bytes) after Wesolowski's attack; SDitH 3.0.0 moved to a VOLEitH-based construction with larger signatures; UOV raised n at every level; SNOVA 3.0 reformulated its public map with rectangular matrices and nine new parameter sets, resolving the wedge-product flags against v2.3; FAEST 3.0.0 shrank signatures 10–15% via a CRT-based VOLE commitment; MQOM v3.0 dropped to 18 parameter sets and added a One-Tree variant; and MAYO2's public key fell from 4912 to 2928 bytes while MAYO1 grew to restore margin against improved MQ attacks.
- **Link:** https://github.com/PQShield/nist-sigs-zoo/pull/56

### [8/10] SQIsign: Algorithm Specifications and Supporting Documentation, Version 3.0
- **Date:** 2026-09-01
- **Authors:** The SQIsign submission team
- **Summary:** The official Round-3 SQIsign specification, replacing the round-2 primes after Wesolowski's and related isogeny-path results. Signature sizes rise across the board (level I 148→200 bytes, level III 224→306, level V 292→406), and the new quaternion-ideal algorithms developed this period are folded into the accompanying implementation. As the only isogeny candidate left in the on-ramp, SQIsign's willingness to absorb a ~35% size increase rather than weaken its security claim is the clearest signal of how the round-3 teams are handling the tweak window.
- **Link:** https://sqisign.org/spec/sqisign-20260901.pdf

### [8/10] Complexity Analysis and Security Implications of the New Isogeny-Path Algorithm
- **Date:** 2026-09-23 (revised; submitted 2026-08-27)
- **Authors:** Maher Mamah (University of Waterloo)
- **Summary:** Evaluates whether Wesolowski's p^(1/3+o(1))-time-and-memory algorithm for the supersingular isogeny-path problem is a practical threat to SQIsign, examining memory trade-offs, parallel variants and quantum versions. The conclusion is mixed: the prohibitive memory requirement offsets the asymptotic advantage over accelerated Delfs–Galbraith at higher security levels, with a real advantage only at NIST level I, though parallelization and Tani's quantum algorithm could change that. This is the analysis that justifies — or bounds — the parameter increase SQIsign 3.0 just took.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1821

### [7/10] Dimension-4 SQIsign at Round-3 Parameters: Sizes and Costs of the Compact Format
- **Date:** 2026-09-26
- **Authors:** Dustin Ray (Anchorage Digital)
- **Summary:** Derives dimension-4 response parameters for the new Round-3 SQIsign primes and measures a Rust implementation, reporting a 142-byte signature with an 84-byte public key at NIST level I versus 200 bytes for the specification's format. The combined key-plus-signature total is the smallest known among post-quantum signatures, but verification costs roughly 10× the dimension-2 variant. It quantifies exactly what SQIsign could recover of its size advantage after the round-3 parameter growth, and at what price.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2221

### [7/10] Cross-Signature Signing-Key Recovery and Domain-Separation Repair for SDitH v2
- **Date:** 2026-09-14 (revised; submitted 2026-08-26)
- **Authors:** José Luis Delgado
- **Summary:** Shows that each hidden VOLE leaf in SDitH v2 exposes a commitment and a public endpoint, and that shared stream structure across signatures enables cross-signature signing-key recovery at 11.23–11.67 bits below exhaustive search for the AES-128/192/256-level parameter sets. The paper also proposes a repair that binds the signature salt and position indices into the domain separation, neutralizing the attack without changing signature size or performance. It lands as SDitH's Round-3 submission moves to a VOLEitH-based design, making the domain-separation hygiene question a live one for the new version.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1808

### [7/10] New algorithms for quaternion ideals in SQIsign
- **Date:** 2026-09-22
- **Authors:** Antonin Leroux (DGA-MI, IRMAR, Université de Rennes), Sina Schaeffler (IBM Research – Zurich, ETH Zurich)
- **Summary:** Generalizes prior quaternion-ideal representation work into a normalized representation with simple, efficient algorithms for the Deuring correspondence when p ≡ 3 mod 4, improving the worst-case integer bound to O(p⁴) — at least O(p²) better than previous bounds. The algorithms are already implemented in the latest SQIsign version submitted to NIST, so this is core Round-3 implementation work rather than speculative research, and it applies to isogeny-based protocols relying on the Deuring correspondence generally.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2153

### [6/10] Improved Complexity Estimates for Underdetermined MQ Systems via Generalized Variable Partitioning
- **Date:** 2026-09-25 (revised)
- **Authors:** Hideki Asanuma, Yilong Chen, Hiroki Furue, Kosuke Sakata, Tsuyoshi Takagi
- **Summary:** Refines complexity estimates for solving underdetermined multivariate quadratic systems using generalized variable partitioning, tightening the baseline against which UOV-family parameter sets are judged. This line of "improved MQ" analysis is cited as the reason MAYO1's Round-3 parameters were raised, so the revision matters for interpreting the new parameter sets rather than as a standalone break. Together with the Schur–Macaulay work it shows the MQ-solving baseline moving under the multivariate candidates mid-round.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1054

### [6/10] A New Prime-Norm Ideal Sampling for SQIsign
- **Date:** 2026-09-23
- **Authors:** Li-Jie Jian (Academia Sinica)
- **Summary:** Observes that inert ideals of a given norm form a circle modulo that norm, and samples them by drawing lines through a fixed point on the circle so that a single random parameter yields each ideal exactly once. The result is a 13.76× speed-up at NIST level I over the existing implementation while preserving uniform output distribution at negligible extra cost. Pure performance recovery for a scheme that just absorbed a parameter increase, and cheap enough to be a plausible implementation change within round 3.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2172

### [6/10] HyperSolver: Asymptotically and Concretely Accelerating the Delfs–Galbraith Attack using Isogeny Ladders
- **Date:** 2026-08-28
- **Authors:** Lorenz Panny, Ryan Rueger, Alessandro Sferlazza, Aleksei Udovenko
- **Summary:** Presents a memoryless Delfs–Galbraith variant that outperforms the previous best by a logarithmic factor asymptotically and is faster concretely, targeting the supersingular endomorphism-ring problem underlying isogeny cryptography. The authors derive concrete bit-security estimates for isogeny primitives including SQIsign. Since Mamah's analysis concludes that Wesolowski's algorithm mostly loses to *accelerated* Delfs–Galbraith at higher security levels, improvements to that baseline directly move the number SQIsign's Round-3 parameters must clear.
- **Link:** https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1823
